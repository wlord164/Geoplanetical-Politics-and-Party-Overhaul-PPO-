Politics and Party Overhaul (PPO)

This Windows patch supports Geoplanetical 1.1.4, Windows Steam depot 940231, manifest 43069630429240561 (app build 26709373). Install Node.js LTS from https://nodejs.org/en/download if needed. Allow about 4 GB of free space on the Steam library drive for backups and staging.

Close Geoplanetical and use Extract All on the PPO installer ZIP. Double-click Install Party Mod.cmd in the extracted folder. Run the new installer again to update a recognized PPO installation, including after Steam Verify Integrity restores the supported game files. Once the installer reports success, start the game through Steam. If Steam discovery fails, find the game folder with Steam's Browse Local Files and pass that folder as a quoted argument to Install Party Mod.cmd.

If a campaign stopped during monthly finance with corporate-profit:working-capital:insufficient-balance, update PPO and load the last save from before the error. PPO reconciles identified working-capital assignments on load. Advance the campaign and save again to persist the repair.

New campaigns start with 12 playable wartime administrations across Sudan, Yemen, Somalia, Libya, Myanmar, the Democratic Republic of the Congo, and Syria. Each uses the game's country inspector, flag, leadership, governing institutions, military, and diplomacy. Libya keeps its opening ceasefire. Existing campaigns retain their saved territorial ownership.

When your country joins the fighting, the game pauses and shows both sides and their goals. Choose View War to inspect it or Continue to resume. Aid and access do not count as joining the fighting. When you play an opposition party, the AI government makes support decisions. If you control the government, you decide.

Open Defense > Historical Conflicts > Wars to browse and search. Select a war to see its goals, available actions, regional control, and recent changes. Select a participant to see a scoped strength estimate and deaths where known. Other scenario groups lists groups outside the live roster when the record includes them. Research and credits are kept with the mod files.

The Political map shows territorial control in opening conflicts and wars that start during play. Faction holdings use the normal country borders and fitted names. Contested areas keep their muted control fill and use the normal dark border style along changes in control. Hover for a brief summary and click for a box showing the primary conflict, known sides, any reported advantage and measured control where available. Expand other conflicts in the box to see overlapping fronts. Eligible factions have an inspector action and a separate zoom action when they have mapped holdings. Confirmed wartime captures use a slightly brighter shade of the controlling side's map color and a muted yellow dashed boundary until settlement.

The Strategic map uses the same control fills and borders. Its diamonds open details about reported areas and fronts. Turn on Ceasefires or Occupations under Conflicts. A ceasefire means fighting is paused. A reported occupation can cover an area whose exact control is unknown. Yellow dashed borders show captured territory. Open a province to check overlapping fronts and see which actions are available.

Documented opening control in Rakhine uses township boundaries for the Arakan Army. Paletwa, Chipwi, Tonzang, Kyaikdon, Goma, Bukavu, Raqqa and Suwayda use mapped town or city boundaries. These holdings have their own province records. The remainder of each original province keeps its own government. Boundary sources are credited in the historical opening-area JSON files. Population shares use existing game weights, stock city records, and 2014 Myanmar census urban counts as scenario weights.

Where withdrawal terms are available, a government involved in the occupation can submit them. An opposition party can petition its government to negotiate. Decisions and War details show both approval estimates. Expand What affects the odds? to see favorable and unfavorable factors. The government uses the higher of your party's public support or best chamber seat share. Party organizing and election gains can improve that first decision. An implemented deal removes that occupation report and its effects. Overlapping wars and agreements keep their own outcomes.

Only opposing combatants can occupy territory in the same war. In a local war, every combatant can occupy only the regions defined for that front, including countries that join after the war starts. Loading an existing save releases occupation outside those regions. Wartime independence or a change of national government requires a decisive military result or an explicit concession. Existing settlements stay in the save.

Dossier portraits marked Illustrative portrait depict fictional people. Where a real leader is documented, their name and role are shown separately. Where an opening leader is unverified, a locally appropriate name appears under Fictional scenario character. These dossier illustrations do not replace people in the campaign simulation.

Sudan's native theatre labels the breakaway Rapid Support Forces on the globe, with its documented banner. Its country profile retains Government of Peace and Unity and shows Mohamed Hamdan Dagalo. A decisive Tasis victory installs that government as the Republic of Sudan. A negotiated white peace retains both wartime authorities. When a new historical state forms, PPO uses its sourced flag when available or the winning faction's banner provisionally. Historical portraits are tied to the specific incumbent. A new generated officeholder uses the game's portrait system. The historical-identities JSON files include image credits and sources.

The English interface uses official country titles for the January 1, 2026 scenario, including Lebanese Republic, Democratic People's Republic of Korea, and United States of America. The list covers 212 playable entries and 25 map-only entries. Places without a separate formal title use their established country or territory name. Custom country names in saves take precedence. Use the short country name or official title in the new-game country search.

The list follows the UK FCDO geographical names index and UN country naming references. Where usage differs, it uses the local title for Afghanistan (Islamic Emirate of Afghanistan), Taiwan (Republic of China), and Northern Cyprus (Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus). The game's January 2026 scenario uses Republic of Nauru, before the later change to Republic of Naoero. These are scenario display labels.

To rename a party in your current country, go to My Party and click Rename Party. The picker starts on your own party. Choose a party, enter its name and optional abbreviation, and save the campaign to keep the change.

In Politics > Electorate, use the Panel width slider and expand a voter bloc. The Vote split lists every party in the estimate with its color, abbreviation, and vote share to up to two decimal places. Hover over an abbreviation to see the full party name. The slider remembers your preferred width for Electorate.

In Politics > Assembly, expand a party to see the four ideology scores on a -100 to +100 scale. My Party shows your declared tradition, founding preset, and recorded founding positions. Choosing a preset and adjusting its scores keeps your chosen tradition. New parties have a single Centrist founding choice. Existing saves retain their previous preset. Law, assent, and constitutional reform results retain the party profile at the time of the ballot. The four scores still feed the existing simulation rules.

In My Party > Party organizing, choose a voter bloc and check the cost, assigned activists, return date, and projected national support. Public meetings run for 21 days, with 30 days between meetings and 90 days before returning to the same bloc. Legal parties without seats receive up to 50% off the cash and activist requirements at 2.5% support or below. The discount tapers out at 5%. Meetings can also build the existing Outreach network up to 32% strength in a compatible bloc.

Back a live, unresolved peaceful mobilization to build support in its affected blocs. Gains depend on real participation and the fit between the party and the movement's demand. Backing runs for up to 14 days. If the movement settles or becomes ineligible, the panel records the earned progress and returns the assigned activists. Backing has a 60-day party cooldown and is available once per movement.

Meetings and backing share a +50% voting-weight cap per party and bloc. Each quote scales with the room under that cap. The panel shows the organizing half-life saved for the campaign, which is 180 days under Standard rules. Bloc gains feed the game's national support calculation.

Sustained pressure from lost jobs, rising prices, outbreaks, or political instability can increase support for opposition parties when confidence in the government is low. Credibility, real organizing capacity, and fit with local voters determine which parties gain. The effect builds over several weeks and eases during recovery. A party benefiting from the shift can recruit more members when its public campaign work completes. Congress considers membership and outreach separately from those changes in national voting intentions.

In My Party, open Party Congress to see estimated ideological factions in an Assembly-style semicircle. Each dot represents one modeled delegate. Congress starts at 100 delegates. The legend names each represented faction. The breakdown also lists named tendencies with no delegates.

Enter a proposed Congress size from 50 to 1,000 delegates or choose a quick pick. The suggested size reflects active party membership and established regional organization. Check each faction's current and proposed representation, whether it would qualify for a representative, and its expected yes and no votes. A faction can gain delegates but still oppose the motion if its share of Congress shrinks. More than half of the current delegates must vote yes. On a motion forecast to fail, you can offer one opposing faction an organizing grant to persuade some of its delegates. The preview shows the cost and extra votes. The party treasury pays the grant only if the motion passes.

Congress also votes on a campaign priority. For 90 days, the chosen project (regional rallies or local investment) costs 20% less cash and the other costs 15% more. These prices apply when you start a new project. The current delegates vote, with factions weighing whether to favor shorter campaigns or lasting party networks.

Each ballot has a 14-day wait before it can be held again. An adopted campaign priority runs its full 90 days before Congress can replace it. An approved resize is saved and seats the new Congress at its scheduled review. The current delegates continue voting until then. If the resize would leave a faction with an accepted commitment without seats, Congress waits for that commitment to resolve or reach its deadline. Existing campaigns keep their current size until a resize takes effect.

Congress shows the party's declared ideology and the political currents represented by its delegates. The semicircle shows one dot per delegate at every size, including one-delegate currents. Policy wings can share an ideology on the chart while holding separate seats and positions in votes and bargaining. Open Faction positions and evidence for the record behind each bloc. Hover or focus an ideology in the legend to highlight its delegates.

Build active membership and durable regional branches, then organize a compatible current in Congress. The cost and branch requirement are shown before submitting. New recruits join that current, and unaffiliated members support the party platform. Electoral polling does not enroll voters as party members. Existing faction terms and accepted commitments can temporarily retain a legacy delegation in a saved campaign.

Party charter votes let delegates choose appointment or election of future faction representatives and determine whether a single established branch has the authority to organize a new current. A seated current with a position on representative independence can also bring a vote on whether executive and cabinet officeholders can start new terms as faction representatives. Current representatives finish their terms. These motions amend party rules. National legislation and constitutional changes still follow the applicable government procedures.

Different traditions can bring different choices to the programme ballot. For example, Orthodox Marxist–Leninist and Reformist Marxist–Leninist delegates may split over state production planning and enterprise autonomy. When a programme is adopted, the law shortlist and campaign promises prioritize feasible proposals that advance it. Each law still follows its regular approval procedure.

Convene a Congress review to organize factions and fill their leadership roles. Factions with at least 5% of awarded delegates can earn a representative under the party charter. The overall party leader has a separate role. Representatives negotiate for their own faction, including the faction closest to the party leader. Click a faction representative’s portrait or name to open their native People profile. To negotiate, select a programme pledge or an organizing grant and check the expected response. The representative may accept the terms, counter, or refuse. Congress applies agreed endorsements to the actual programme vote.

An accepted organizing grant reserves the agreed cash. Adopting the programme delivers the grant and recruits members. A breach releases the reserve and reduces trust. The faction history records the outcome and the reason. Bills and public offices follow their existing legislative and appointment procedures.

Scheduled reviews can adjust faction representation as factions organize or lose members. When a sponsored bill advances the active programme, the next review recognizes delivery and can shift issue priorities.

Staff can negotiate if you enable the bargaining mandate in Congress. Choose permitted subjects, a monthly spending limit, and a minimum treasury reserve.

Open My Party > Law Shortlist to see changes that move current laws closer to your party's position. The list puts proposals forecast to pass first, followed by the closest votes. Compare the proposed change, estimated votes, and any veto or court risk. Refresh to recalculate. Review proposal opens the game’s law screen with the suggested change selected. Your party must still meet the normal rules for introducing a bill.

Open My Party > Uprising as a legal opposition party in an eligible sovereign country. You can prepare and declare while your country is in an outside war. The declaration shows your starting region, constitution, name choices, and founding laws. The starting region must be outside the capital and share a land border with the rest of the country.

Open the Uprising Map to compare regional footholds on a fixed color scale. The region list shows exact percentages. Use the Projected Control layer to see where the government and breakaway would start, and look for the outlined party headquarters. Foothold comes from local vote share, permanent regional presence, headquarters, and completed wing investments.

To declare, establish your party headquarters in an eligible region outside the capital, build at least 60% foothold there, and finish training at least one division. You can establish or relocate the same headquarters from the Uprising tab. The previous headquarters stays open until a relocation finishes. Form the party wing to unlock regional foothold investments and division training. Up to two regional investments run at once. Each takes 21 days and adds up to 20 percentage points below 50% foothold or 16 points from 50% onward. Connected regions at 60% can join at declaration. The incumbent keeps the capital and at least 20% of the population.

The wing can initially prepare two divisions. Each eligible region at 30% foothold adds one slot, and an eligible headquarters at 60% adds another. Establish an eligible party headquarters to receive equipment. Choose infantry, motorized, mechanized, or artillery and select Prepare Division to pay for recruitment and schedule a kit delivery. The quote shows the equipment, costs, delivery date, and ready date. Basic kits are always available once you have an eligible headquarters. Accept a grant in My Party > Foreign Funding to unlock Standard kits, which cost more and take longer. Foreign military aid remains separate. Each accepted aid credit reduces the next division's treasury cost by $24,000.

Recruited activists permanently leave the party pool and count toward their division's staffing from the joining regions' available personnel. Delivered kits are reserved in the HQ stockpile and equip divisions when they deploy at headquarters. Pending deliveries wait if your HQ becomes unusable and transfer to your next eligible HQ. At declaration, an arrived kit for an unfinished division goes into military reserve. Understrength divisions form while awaiting reinforcements. If the breakaway wins, surviving land divisions join the reunited country's military with their current personnel and equipment. Preparations carry over in existing saves, including previously trained infantry. Wars already underway continue with their existing territories and units.

The breakaway uses the party's current ideology. Its system determines the constitution, assembly rules, and founding laws. A breakaway that uses the same system as the country started with uses a Revolutionary Government name during the civil war and restores the starting title if it wins. Other breakaways keep their chosen names. Use Nation Identity to change your flag during the war. If you win, the new government keeps the breakaway flag and charter. A multiparty outcome preserves existing assembly seats until an election. An incumbent victory bans the founding party.

After an uprising victory, open Defense > Historical Conflicts > Decisions. Use Seek peace to review the terms and send an offer to the other side. Your country remains in the war while the offer is reviewed. If the other side agrees, review the terms again and accept to sign. Other combatants can keep fighting after a separate peace. Keep fighting closes the post-uprising decision for that war. Decisions also lists voluntary military aid the new government can end and occupation proposals or petitions you can review.

Open Support Base beside Party Congress to see which electorate blocs contribute the most to your party's estimated voters. The bar and large percentage show the bloc's share of your party's support. The smaller percentage shows your party's estimated support within that bloc.

Open My Party > Campaign Plan to select a contest, compare the current estimate with the election-day projection, and see what work is underway. The projection uses current world conditions and expected effects of booked projects. The election recap records the most recent completed legislative result in each chamber. The Agenda links to upcoming work and decisions.

You can delegate routine party work in Campaign Plan with a goal, a spending limit, and a cash reserve. A party's organization pays the same costs and observes the same requirements whether you, a delegated plan, or the AI directs it. Completed organizing and local projects can develop a dues-paying membership. Membership can shrink when a party remains inactive. Campaign Plan shows the review schedule and latest staff action. Expand Party activity below the plan to see decisions, cash flows and activist assignments and returns. New entries caused by staff are marked Staff.

Open My Party > Regional Campaigning and select a region on the inline map or in the Region menu. Compare population share, cost, completion date, and estimated gains before starting a rally, investment, or headquarters. The panel follows project progress and activist return dates at your chosen game speed. Use Open Campaign Map for the optional globe view. A regional investment builds lasting electoral presence. Invest in Foothold in the Uprising tab prepares the region for an uprising.

A rally takes seven days. New rallies hold their full boost for 21 days, then halve every 60 days. A local investment takes 42 days and builds lasting party presence. The quoted odds determine whether growth is strong or modest. Established regions gain less presence from each new investment. Presence adds local voting weight and lowers future rally costs in that region.

Your party can establish one headquarters. It takes 28 days to open and adds 12% to your party's local voting weight while the headquarters is there. Voting weight is normalized against other parties' support, so this is a smaller change in actual vote share. The panel shows the resulting change in percentage points. Regional gains feed elections using regional results and contribute by population to nationwide voting. Projects, investment outcomes and headquarters locations are saved with the campaign.

The regional panel shows projected national support, seat prospects, and the applicable electoral rules. Project previews estimate the change on completion if other conditions hold. The Campaign operation previews the marginal vote and seat change for the selected contest alongside cost and caps. Regional projects assign activists until completion. Election operations spend them.

Open My Party > Foreign Funding to request a one-time grant from a party in another country. The offer preview shows ideology, solidarity, electoral traction, and the donor treasury limit. Closely aligned parties are more willing to fund new parties. Authoritarian-left parties can have additional solidarity based on their economic and governance positions. A donor still needs enough money to make an offer. You can have one request or deal active, with 120 days between approaches. Review the amount, terms, and exposure risk before accepting. Accepted funds go directly into your party treasury.

Declared backing is public and costs a small amount of party credibility. Private backing can be exposed within 90 days. If that happens, your party loses credibility and has 30 days to return the unspent portion or keep the grant. Returning funds reduces the additional credibility loss. Party spending reduces the returnable balance, and later fundraising does not restore it. Requests, offers, and grants are saved with the campaign.

Rival parties consider the same organizing projects during the game's monthly party decision cycle. Their decisions account for available cash, activists, influence, and the next election.

From New Game, select a country and open Campaign Rules on the role screen. Standard keeps the usual pace and helps small parties slightly. Easy gives a newly created party more resources and support, lowers campaign costs for everyone, and uses simpler AI planning. Competitive uses stronger AI planning, faster decay, and local presence upkeep. Change any setting to use Custom. The game saves campaign rules with the campaign.

Choose Create Party to found a new party. Set its name and ideology before starting. Funding, activists, influence, national support, regional presence, and opening seats apply once to the new party. Standard starts with zero seats and up to 2.5% support. The available party roster may lower the support target. Custom starting seats come from the principal elected chamber where eligible. Its total seats stay fixed, and the new party starts outside government. When an opposition party declares an uprising, it gives up its seats in the original legislature. Each chamber redistributes those seats among its remaining eligible parties using D’Hondt, which favors larger parties.

Player and AI parties use the same campaign quotes, cooldowns, and decay rules. In Regional Campaigning or on the Campaign Map, open Support Breakdown for a region. It shows each party's baseline, local presence, headquarters, rally gains, and the effect of rival campaigns. Existing saves keep their founding position and existing project terms.

Islamic Democracy, Islamic Republicanism, Islamic Fundamentalism, Fascism, Maoism, Democratic Confederalism, Anarcho-Syndicalism and Ecosocialism add distinct platforms. Their law priorities appear in the Law Shortlist as the game can forecast a valid proposal. They supply founding institutions and laws when the party wins a declared uprising. In historical conflicts, specific factions' names, mandates, and laws come from their own opening and settlement records.

The native executive model allows one officeholder per office. Democratic Confederalism uses a confederal structure with constitutional civil protections. Anarcho-Syndicalism uses a confederal structure with protected unions and strikes. Co-leadership and worker control of individual firms are represented in the documented program.

To remove the patch, close Geoplanetical and run Restore Original Game.cmd. Steam updates can replace the patched files. The installer checks the supported game files before making any changes. The restore command keeps newer Steam files if they have replaced the patched files.

Existing modded campaigns retain organizing bonuses, cooldowns, endorsement history, and ongoing organizing projects.

Custom party persistence: the game can automatically merge small, similar parties or remove a party after prolonged low support. This update protects custom parties from those two automatic removal rules, including custom parties in older saves. They can still lose support, lose elections, and remain in opposition. If a party has already disappeared from a save, load an earlier save where the party still exists after installing the update.
